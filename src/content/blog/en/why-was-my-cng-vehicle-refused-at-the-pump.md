Has a CNG fuel pump attendant refused to refill your car, taxi, or commercial vehicle? 

In Haryana, Delhi-NCR, and across India, City Gas Distribution (CGD) fuel stations strictly enforce statutory safety regulations. If an attendant inspects your vehicle and turns you away, one of the primary reasons is that your **CNG cylinder's testing validity has expired, or the required compliance marking plate is missing or damaged**.

CNG cylinders store methane at extreme pressures up to 200 bar (approx. 2,900 PSI). To safeguard motorists, fuel attendants, and the public from catastrophic rupture hazards, applicable safety regulations mandate that all onboard CNG fuel cylinders undergo periodic hydrostatic stretch testing every three years.

Here is an actionable step-by-step guide explaining why this happens and what you should do immediately.

---

## Why Do CNG Stations Deny Refueling?

Under Rule 35 of the Gas Cylinders Rules and Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) automotive safety directives, fuel dispensers must verify testing compliance before attaching high-pressure refueling nozzles.

Common reasons for fuel refusal include:
1. **Expired Compliance Plate:** The 3-year statutory validity stamped on your brass compliance plate has passed.
2. **Missing Compliance Plate:** The stamped brass plate near the filling valve or on the cylinder collar has fallen off or was never fitted.
3. **Mismatched Vehicle or Cylinder Details:** The cylinder serial number does not match vehicle registration records.
4. **Physical Cylinder Damage:** Visible corrosion, denting, or non-approved aftermarket piping.

---

## Step 1 – Check Your Cylinder's Testing Details

Do not guess your validity. You can check your cylinder status in less than two minutes:

1. **Locate the Brass Compliance Plate:** Open your fuel filler flap or bonnet. Most authorized retrofitters install a stamped brass plate adjacent to the CNG refilling receptacle or on the cylinder mounting collar.
2. **Inspect the Stamped Date:** The plate displays the last hydrostatic test date (Month/Year) and the retest due date.
3. **Verify Cylinder Collar Markings:** The neck or collar of the steel cylinder itself is permanently stamped with the original manufacturing test date, tare weight, and working pressure (typically 200 bar / 300 bar test).

## Step 2 – Confirm Whether Your Testing is Overdue

In India, statutory testing is required every **3 years (36 months)** from the last certified test date. 

- **If your plate shows a past date:** Your cylinder is legally overdue. Fuel stations are prohibited by law from filling your tank until certified retesting is completed.
- **If your plate is within 60 days of expiry:** Arrange an inspection promptly to prevent being stranded with an empty tank.

## Step 4 – What Happens During Certified Testing?

Certified testing typically takes **2 to 3 hours**. During this procedure:
1. **Residual Gas Degassing:** Controlled methane evacuation under safe, explosion-proof conditions.
2. **Internal Endoscopy & Ultrasonic Wall Scanning:** Inspecting the cylinder walls for rust pitting, cracks, and metal loss.
3. **300 Bar Water Jacket Expansion Test:** Pressurizing the cylinder with water to 1.5 times working pressure to measure permanent volumetric expansion. Cylinders with permanent stretch below 10% pass.
4. **Hot-Air Internal Baking:** Dehydrating internal surfaces to eliminate moisture and prevent internal rust.
5. **New Stamped Brass Plate & Certificate:** Mounting the new compliance plate near your filling valve and issuing an official test certificate for fuel pump acceptance.
